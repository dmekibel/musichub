How everything relates

Music is shapes on a circle, moving through time. Pitch is a circle; rhythm is a circle. One geometry, measured the same way for every piece, rolled up to artists, styles, places and decades. Then we do research with it, the way ColorHub does with color.

The Five Dials

The Fourier transform of the notes measures how much music leans toward each symmetric shape on the 12-note clock. Each dial has a strength and a direction. One piece of math holds Bach's keys, Barry Harris's squares, Coltrane's triangles and Debussy's hexagons.

Bach's Prelude in C, bar by bar (computed live from the score in this page)

Squares hits 1.00 exactly where Bach writes a diminished chord. And a plain G7 already reads 0.86 square: Barry Harris's "a dominant is a diminished chord with one note moved", as a number. Over the whole piece the squares cancel to almost zero, because Bach uses all three.

Shape twins

Because scales and rhythms are both shapes on a circle, the same shape shows up as a scale in one culture and a rhythm in another.

The major scale7 notes out of 12
The bembé bell patternWest Africa: 7 strokes in 12 pulses
The bossa nova clave5 hits spread as evenly as possible over 16

The major scale and the bembé bell are the same shape. Jeff Pressing showed it in 1983. In MusicHub, shapes get their own pages, so the scale, the bell and every piece that uses either are one tap apart.

Research with the archive

Become the archive, measure everything the same way, then publish findings, each with its chart, n, method and the pieces behind every dot. Planned studies:

  1. The shape of a century. The Five Dials of every decade: when squares rose, when hexagons peaked.
  2. The most square composer. Barry Harris's world, ranked by measure.
  3. Shape twins across cultures. Every pitch shape that's also a rhythm somewhere.
  4. Signature moves. Each artist's most characteristic chord moves, weighted by how unusual they are.
  5. Story shapes of music. Every tension curve sorted into a few archetypes, like the emotional arcs of novels.
  6. How shocking was it then. Surprise measured against music of its own year.
  7. Voice-leading economy. Who moves the voices least. Bach's Prelude: 82% of moves are a step or less.
  8. The harmonic color of the world. The map colored by harmony, decade by decade.
  9. Progression migration. The ii–V–I and the Andalusian cadence travelling the map.
  10. Gravity. How far each style dares to wander from home.
  11. Instrument keys. Guitar music lives in E and A, horn music in B♭ and E♭.
  12. Sad words in major keys. Lyrics against harmony, once lyrics are licensed.
