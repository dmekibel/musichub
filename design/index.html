Four directions

The same moment in all four: bar 12 of Bach's Prelude in C, a C♯ diminished seventh over G. Real data, real chord, real story. Tap one to open it full screen. These choose the look; the deeper visualizations come next.

1 · my pick for the player

Nocturne

The music is the interface. Black, and the room glows the color of the harmony; the chord hangs in the dark as points of light.

After Apple Music's lyric view, Brian Eno's Bloom, Apple Watch faces, glowing-piano videos.

Immersive, calm, full screen, works for anyone. The glow changing with home, away and tension is the signature. Risk: dark apps can look generic without that signature.

2 · the most "expensive"

Edition

A scholarly edition you can hear. Cream paper, real engraved notation, analysis set like an Urtext, color only as pigment ink.

After Henle and Bärenreiter editions, Tufte's books, Farrow & Ball cards, Aesop and Kinfolk.

Instant credibility with musicians; shows how the voices move (C♯ rises to D). Risk: notation can scare people who don't read music.

3 · the most original

Kandinsky

The piece paints itself. Every bar takes its place on a wheel (shape = function, size = tension) and the bass spirals in toward home.

After Kandinsky's Composition VIII and his Bauhaus color-shape test, Fischinger, Fantasia.

The finished painting is a poster and the piece's chip in the archive. Risk: harder to read moment to moment; best as the ending, not the player.

4 · the boldest

Blue Note

Every moment is a record cover: one flat field of color, giant type off the edge, the chord as one bold shape.

After Reid Miles's Blue Note covers, Müller-Brockmann's grids, Teenage Engineering.

Striking in a screenshot and great for jazz. Risk: loud; a full-screen color that flips every bar could tire people over two minutes.

My recommendation

One system, not four: Nocturne as the look. Edition's real notation becomes a "Score" lens inside it (cream ink on black). Kandinsky's wheel becomes what you get when a piece ends: its poster, and its chip in the archive wall.
